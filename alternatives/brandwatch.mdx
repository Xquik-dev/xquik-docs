## Where Brandwatch fits

Brandwatch sells enterprise social listening and consumer intelligence across channels. It outputs dashboards, segments, reports, alerts, and suite exports. Read this page when you compare a broad research suite with an X-only API.

This guide compares Brandwatch and Xquik for the X part of a workflow. That can be a social listening, consumer intelligence, or social media management workflow. Compare tasks like tweet search, follower export, account or keyword monitoring, webhooks, and handoffs. A handoff sends results to apps or agents.

<Info>

Before buying, verify current pricing, access rules, and product terms on Brandwatch's official site.

</Info>

## Source-backed Brandwatch scope

Brandwatch's official consumer intelligence pages describe a suite for searching conversations and segmenting audiences. It also covers analyzing trends with AI and sharing insights through alerts and reports. Their public pages also describe historical and real-time data coverage. They describe official firehose access to Twitter, Tumblr, and Reddit too. They list dashboards, audience demographics, influencers, image analysis, and Signals alerts. They also list Excel, PPT & PDF exports and Brandwatch API access.

Brandwatch's official social media management pages describe one suite. It covers a content calendar, publishing workflows, approval flows, and an Engage inbox. It also covers sentiment and spam detection, helpdesk integration, campaign reporting, and social listening.

Use those official pages to verify the broad-suite side of the comparison. Use Xquik's API reference and workflow guides to verify the X-specific side. That side covers tweet search, follower exports, monitors, webhooks, exports, SDKs, and MCP.

## What to test in Xquik

For social listening comparisons, run the trial on the exact job you need. That job can be a tweet, profile, follower, reply, or timeline job. Compare returned records, export format, webhook delivery, API behavior, and cost.

Start with tweet search, follower exports, monitor events, or signed webhook payloads. CSV, JSON & XLSX exports, SDK calls, or MCP tool results also work.

  <Card title="Search tweets" icon="search">

## What to verify in a trial

<Steps>

  <Step title="Run one social listening job">

    Pick 1 real query. Monitor a brand account, search tweets for a campaign term, or export followers. Or send new matching tweets to a webhook.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect the returned data">

    Compare tweet IDs, author IDs, timestamps, text, metrics, and media links. Also compare pagination, export fields, webhook signatures, and error handling.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Price the handoff">

    Compare Brandwatch package requirements with Xquik credits, active monitor billing, and top-ups. Include the engineering time needed to keep the workflow running.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Migration path

Start with one X-only workflow and one owner for its output. Leave the full listening program in place.

1. Brandwatch covers broad listening dashboards and team reporting. Keep using it if your team already works that way.
2. Use Xquik for the X task that needs API output. Examples are tweet search, follower export, tweet monitoring, webhook delivery, SDK calls, and MCP.
3. Keep both systems side by side until the Xquik output fits the receiving system. It must match the fields, freshness, and format that system needs.

Price the real workload. On Xquik, Starter is USD 20/month with 140,000 credits. Top-ups are USD 0.00015/credit. Webhook and stored-event management cost no credits. Active monitors bill only while enabled.
